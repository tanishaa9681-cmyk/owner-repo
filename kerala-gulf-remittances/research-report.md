# Iran war squeezes Kerala's ₹2 lakh-crore lifeline

**Bottom line (as of 28 Sept 2026):** The 2026 Iran war has not yet cut the money Kerala receives. It is cutting what that money rests on: Gulf jobs, flights and the stock of workers. Headline data still look healthy, and even strong. India's private transfers were **$42.9 bn in Apr–Jun 2026 against $33.2 bn a year earlier (about +29%)**, and Kerala's NRI deposits reached **₹3.24 lakh crore on 31 Mar 2026**. That is the "false comfort" trap. Frightened migrants are bringing their savings home, and a record-weak rupee (**₹96.61/$ on 19 May 2026**) inflates the rupee value of every transfer. The inflow that best tracks ordinary workers' savings, **NRE deposits, fell from $2.42 bn to $0.21 bn in Apr–Jul 2026 (about −91%)**. Kerala is exposed because it receives about **₹2.17 lakh crore a year (KMS 2023), roughly 19.7% of India's remittances**, and about **80.5% of its emigrants work in the GCC**. The benchmark **20% fall is a projection by S. Irudaya Rajan (Apr 2026), not an observed number**. On the 2023 base it would take about **₹43,000 crore a year** out of household incomes, roughly **2.7% of 2026–27 GSDP**. Kerala has lived through this before: the 1990 Kuwait airlift, Nitaqat in 2013 and 15 lakh COVID returnees. Each time, the Centre ran the evacuation, the state paid relief, and reintegration stayed tiny. The 2026 budget repeats the pattern, announcing new schemes with **no disclosed allocations**. Keralites will keep going anyway. Youth unemployment is **29% (national: 10.2%)** and female youth unemployment **47%**, even though Kerala pays India's highest manual wages.

## Gulf money is about a fifth of Kerala's income, and the headline figure overstates it

**Scale [C]**
- **Total remittances were ₹2,16,893 crore in 2023, up 154.9% from ₹85,092 crore in 2018** (Kerala Migration Survey 2023, IIMAD/GIFT, 20,000 households) ([KMS 2023](https://iimad.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/KMS-2023-Report.pdf)). That is roughly **₹594 crore a day** [Calc].
- Remittances equalled **23.2% of NSDP in 2023 (13.5% in 2018)** and **1.7x the state's revenue receipts**. Kerala held **21% of India's NRI deposits** ([KMS 2023](https://iimad.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/KMS-2023-Report.pdf)).
- **The RBI view gives the same order of magnitude.** Kerala received **19.7% of India's $118.7 bn** in inward remittances in 2023–24, second only to Maharashtra at 20.5%. Kerala has about 3% of India's population ([FLAME/The Hindu, 6 Apr 2026](https://www.flame.edu.in/in-the-media/kerala-likely-to-see-20-drop-in-remittances-as-west-asia-war-drags-on); [Onmanorama, May 2025](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/business/2025/05/29/nri-remittance-record-kerala-deposits.html); [Dhanam, 17 Mar 2026](https://english.dhanamonline.com/banking-and-finance/war-shrinks-gulf-remittances-to-india-kerala-feels-the-heat-9002932)).
- **Remittances per receiving household were ₹2.24 lakh in 2023**, against ₹96,185 in 2018 ([StudyIQ summary of KMS 2023](https://www.studyiq.com/articles/kerala-migration-survey/)).

**Concentration: why a Gulf shock hits Kerala harder than India [C]**
- **GCC share of Kerala's emigrants (2023): 80.5%.** By country: UAE 38.6%, Saudi Arabia 16.9%, Qatar 9.1%, Oman 6.4%, Kuwait 5.8%, Bahrain 3.7%. The GCC share was 89.2% in 2018 ([KMS 2023](https://iimad.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/KMS-2023-Report.pdf)).
- **The GCC supplies only 37.9% of India's remittances (2023–24)**, down from 46.7% in 2016–17. Advanced economies now supply more than half, with the US alone at 27.7% ([InsightsIAS summary of RBI Bulletin](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2025/04/05/rbis-remittances-survey-2025/)). **India has diversified away from the Gulf. Kerala has not, to the same degree.**
- **Geography:** Malappuram has the most emigrants (**3.78 lakh, 17.5%, in 2023**), followed by Thrissur, Kannur, Kollam and Kozhikode ([KMS 2023](https://iimad.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/KMS-2023-Report.pdf)). By receipts, **Kollam takes 17.8% and Malappuram 16.2%** ([CPPR, 6 Aug 2026](https://www.cppr.in/archives/kerala-after-2026-gcc-crisis)).
- **Community:** **Muslims are 41.9% of emigrants**, against about 26% of the population. Hindus are 35.2% and Christians 22.3% ([KMS 2023](https://iimad.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/KMS-2023-Report.pdf)).
- **Worker profile:** **76.9% are labour migrants.** Top occupations are sales (12.9%), transport (11.0%) and construction (2.7%) ([CPPR](https://www.cppr.in/archives/kerala-after-2026-gcc-crisis)). These are the service jobs the war is hitting.

**The banking layer [C]**
- **Kerala's NRI deposits were ₹3.24 lakh crore on 31 Mar 2026.** Federal Bank held ₹92,326 cr, SBI ₹84,967 cr, South Indian Bank ₹31,947 cr and Canara ₹23,352 cr ([Hindusthan Samachar, 18 Jul 2026](https://english.hindusthansamachar.in/Encyc/2026/7/18/Gulf-Keralites-remittances.php)).
- Deposits crossed ₹1 lakh crore in Dec 2014 and ₹2 lakh crore in Mar 2020 ([FLAME media](https://www.flame.edu.in/in-the-media/nri-deposits-in-kerala-banks-head-towards-rs-3-trillion-mark)).

**Caveats [P]**
- **23.2% of NSDP is a KMS survey estimate.** On GSDP the ratio is lower: ₹2.17 lakh cr ÷ ₹14.27 lakh cr (2025–26 BE) ≈ **15.2%**, and ÷ ₹16.29 lakh cr (2026–27) ≈ **13.3%** [Calc] ([PRS](https://prsindia.org/budgets/states/kerala-budget-analysis-2025-26); [FLAME/The Hindu](https://www.flame.edu.in/in-the-media/kerala-likely-to-see-20-drop-in-remittances-as-west-asia-war-drags-on)).
- Part of the 2018→2023 jump comes from method. KMS 2023 was the first digital round and used a larger sample. The rupee also depreciated over the period ([IJLE 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41027-020-00280-z)).
- **Assumption:** the GCC share of remittance *value* is below the 80.5% share of *emigrants*. Western emigrants earn more, so the "80% of inflows from the Gulf" line is an approximation.

**Takeaway:** by any measure, Kerala runs a remittance economy. Present the dependence as "13–23% of state income, depending on the measure", not as a flat 23%.

## The war hits Kerala through jobs, flights and the rupee, not through the transfers themselves

### What "the war" is [C]
- **28 Feb 2026:** the US and Israel struck Iran and killed Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei. **Iran retaliated against Israel and against GCC states that host US forces:** the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain and Oman. It also choked the Strait of Hormuz ([Wikipedia: 2026 Iran war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war); [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/event/2026-Iran-war)).
- **Key dates (2026):**
  - **8 Apr:** ceasefire.
  - **17 Jun:** Islamabad Memorandum lifts the blockade.
  - **8 Jul:** the truce collapses ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war)).
  - **7 Sept:** Saudi Aramco's Jizan refinery is hit for the second time in a month, and only **about 10 commodity ships a day** are crossing Hormuz ([Al Jazeera, 7 Sep 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/9/7/oil-prices-surge-as-us-iran-strikes-intensify-in-strait-of-hormuz)).
  - **23 Sept:** an Indian seafarer is killed on the UAE–India cargo ship *Cape Dao*, and the US walks out during Iran's speech at the UN General Assembly ([The Week, 23 Sep 2026](https://www.theweek.in/news/middle-east/2026/09/23/indian-among-2-seafarers-killed-in-hormuz-ship-attack-amid-us-iran-spat-at-unga-2026.html)).
- **Status on 28 Sept 2026:** there is no working ceasefire. The war has settled into a maritime and energy conflict. Whether talks will resume is unconfirmed.

**Why this shock differs from 2015–16:** that downturn came from *low* oil, which starved Gulf budgets. This one pairs *high* oil with physical disruption. That hits the labour-intensive sectors where Keralites work (hospitality, aviation, retail, construction) even while Gulf treasuries fill. (This is inference from the evidence above.)

### State revenue: an indirect hit, landing on a weak balance sheet
**The mechanism.** Kerala cannot tax remittances directly. The damage comes through spending that remittances pay for, which the state does tax:
- SGST on consumption (a destination-based GST favours a consuming state like Kerala)
- stamp duty and registration on land and housing
- motor vehicle tax
- taxes on liquor and fuel
- lottery receipts

This is an analytical framework; the notes did not retrieve an itemised breakdown of the state's own tax revenue.

**Illustrative first-round hit [Calc].**
- Assumption: the lost spending is taxed at about the state's own-tax-to-GSDP ratio of **6.9%** ([NITI Aayog, Mar 2025](https://www.niti.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-03/Macro-and-Fiscal-Landscape-of-the-State-of-Kerala.pdf)).
- On that assumption, a 20% fall costs the treasury about **₹3,000 crore a year directly**.
- Treat this as a **floor**. It ignores multiplier effects on construction, real estate and retail.

**The fiscal starting point [C].**
- Aggregate debt is **₹5.07 lakh crore**.
- CPPR projects a **₹20,500 crore revenue shortfall in 2026–27** ([CPPR, 6 Aug 2026](https://www.cppr.in/archives/kerala-after-2026-gcc-crisis)).
- Public debt was **38.2% of GSDP** in 2022–23, and committed spending takes **54.9% of expenditure** ([NITI Aayog](https://www.niti.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-03/Macro-and-Fiscal-Landscape-of-the-State-of-Kerala.pdf)).
- The fiscal deficit for 2025–26 (BE) was **₹45,039 cr (3.2% of GSDP)** ([PRS](https://prsindia.org/budgets/states/kerala-budget-analysis-2025-26)).

**The real risk is to households, not the treasury.** The direct fiscal loss is modest. The concentrated damage is to incomes in Malappuram, Kollam and Kannur, and it arrives just as the state has the least fiscal room to cushion it.

## Early crisis data flatter Kerala: the false-comfort trap

**The core insight:** in a crisis, measured remittance *inflows* often **rise** even as the future remittance *flow* is being destroyed. Three forces cause this:
1. **Savings repatriation.** Workers who expect to lose their jobs send home everything they have saved.
2. **A weak rupee.** Each dirham buys more rupees, so the same transfer looks bigger.
3. **Deposit parking.** Returnees move lump sums into NRE and FCNR accounts.

**2026 evidence of rising inflows [C]:**
- **The early-March surge was "20–30% above normal, driven by fear, not prosperity"** ([The Quint, 3 Apr 2026](https://www.thequint.com/opinion/gulf-war-casts-a-long-shadow-over-kerala-assembly-elections)).
- Exchange houses reported a **4–5% dip in transaction counts, with larger average ticket sizes** (mid-Mar 2026) ([Dhanam, 17 Mar 2026](https://english.dhanamonline.com/banking-and-finance/war-shrinks-gulf-remittances-to-india-kerala-feels-the-heat-9002932)). Fewer senders sending bigger sums is exactly what savings being liquidated looks like.
- **Q1 FY27 private transfers were $42.9 bn, against $33.2 bn a year earlier (+29%)** ([secondary summary of the RBI BoP release, 1 Sep 2026](https://www.aparnadecors.com/2026/09/indias-current-account-deficit-widens.html); [Clarity UPSC](https://clarityupsc.com/news/blitz/india-s-current-account-deficit-widened-to-4-2-billion-in-april-june-2026-12)). This needs checking against the RBI's primary release.
- At end-Mar 2026, **Federal Bank's NRI deposits were up 7% QoQ** and South Indian Bank's up 4.2% ([The Wire, 8 May 2026](https://m.thewire.in/article/banking/in-wake-of-depreciating-rupee-foreign-remittances-to-kerala-remain-unaffected-despite-west-asia-crisis)).

**The tell [C]:**
- India's NRI deposit inflows jumped **more than 6x to $36.24 bn in Apr–Jul FY27**. But **$34.53 bn of that was FCNR(B)** money, drawn by an RBI concessional swap window opened in June 2026. That is arbitrage by wealthier NRIs.
- **NRE inflows, the account most tied to ordinary workers' savings, collapsed from $2.42 bn to $207 mn** ([Business Standard, 27 Sep 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/nri-deposit-flows-surge-over-6x-to-36-2-bn-in-apr-jul-fy27-rbi-data-126092700465_1.html)).
- Put simply, **deposits of the rich rose, and deposits of the workers dried up.**

**The precedent (COVID, 2020–21) [C]:**
- **15,01,326 NRIs returned to Kerala** between May 2020 and 3 Jul 2021, and **10.73 lakh of them said they had lost their jobs** ([Onmanorama, 6 Jul 2021, citing NORKA](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/kerala/2021/07/06/nri-return-expats-covid-gulf-norka-data.html)).
- **Yet Kerala's NRI deposits rose 14% in FY21, from ₹1.998 to ₹2.274 lakh crore**, because returnees deposited their savings ([Business Today, 29 Jun 2021](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/economy-politics/story/nri-bank-deposits-to-kerala-rise-14-at-rs-227-lakh-crore-in-fy21-299939-2021-06-29)).
- **More than 70% of surveyed returnees said their remittances rose** after COVID ([World Bank blog, Kerala Return Emigrant Survey 2021](https://blogs.worldbank.org/peoplemove/kerala-return-emigrant-survey-2021-insights-and-way-forward)).
- **Then the bill arrived.** Kerala's share of India's remittances in the RBI survey fell from 19% (2016–17) to 10.2% (2020–21). It recovered to 19.7% only by 2023–24 ([Onmanorama, 18 Jul 2022](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/business/2022/07/18/nri-remittance-share-india.html)). Part of that fall reflects RBI's method of attributing money to the state where the receiving bank sits.

**The bankers' own caveat [C]:** Federal Bank MD K.V.S. Manian said in May 2026: "Unless you see significant job losses and Indians returning for good... I don't think this story is likely to change immediately" ([Khaleej Times, 19 May 2026](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/asia/india-rupee-hits-record-low-but-gulf-remittances-stay-strong-analysts-say)). His *unless* is the whole thesis.

**Inference:** if the maritime war and the UAE services slump persist, expect the real decline to show up **2–4 quarters after the shock**, in the data for H2 FY27 and FY28. Strong inflow numbers in the first two or three quarters should be read as liquidation of savings, not as resilience.

## Keralites went to the Gulf because land reform, caste and joblessness met the 1973 oil boom

**Old ties [C]**
- Malabar's trade with the Gulf dates to the 1st century AD (Pattanam excavations). Omani sailors married into Calicut families ([Ilias, OMER Journal 2018](https://omerjournal.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/vol-2-no-1-trinity-2018-ilias.pdf)).
- By the late 1800s Malayalis were already working in Ceylon, Burma, Aden, Basra and Penang ([Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/1068343/the-malayali-on-the-moon-and-fault-lines-of-caste-what-drove-generations-of-migration-from-kerala)).

**Push factors at home [C]**
- **Land reform:** the 1957 Communist government's land-reform push broke up large estates. Upper-caste families lost their agrarian base and went looking for work across India and beyond ([Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/1068343/the-malayali-on-the-moon-and-fault-lines-of-caste-what-drove-generations-of-migration-from-kerala)).
- **Caste:** for lower-caste migrants, work abroad was hard but free of "the contempt that they did back home" ([Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/1068343/the-malayali-on-the-moon-and-fault-lines-of-caste-what-drove-generations-of-migration-from-kerala)).
- **Unemployment:** in the KMS sample, **58.8% of emigrants had jobs before migrating and 85.8% after**. Migration worked as Kerala's safety valve for unemployment ([CDS WP 483](https://cds.edu/wp-content/uploads/WP483.pdf)).
- **Unions (contested):** nokkukooli ("wages for looking on") is often blamed for weak industrialisation ([Wikipedia: Nokku kooli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nokku_kooli)). Present it as one disputed strand, not the cause.

**Pull factor [C]:** researchers call emigration after the 1973 oil boom "a watershed", Kerala's largest international migration stream ([IJLE 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41027-020-00280-z)).

**The dhow years [C]**
- Launches ran from Bombay to Khor Fakkan or Sohar in 9 to 22 days. On one 22-day trip, **fourteen passengers died**. Agents in Bombay charged about **₹400** ([Ilias](https://omerjournal.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/vol-2-no-1-trinity-2018-ilias.pdf)).
- The "kalla kappal" (thief ship) dropped passengers offshore, and they swam to land ([The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/kerala/how-boat-journey-mid-1960s-started-kerala-s-gulf-boom-130847)).
- **Story, Shahjahan:** he sold his sister's jewellery to buy a ₹5,000 NOC and reached Dubai in 1975. He earned 1,000 dirhams a month, sent home 500, and was jailed in the 1978 visa crackdown ([The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/kerala/how-boat-journey-mid-1960s-started-kerala-s-gulf-boom-130847)).
- **Story, Yusuff Ali (Lulu):** he landed in Dubai on **31 Dec 1973 at age 18** after six days on the *Dumra*. Lulu now has **260+ hypermarkets and malls in 24 countries and more than $8.4 bn in revenue** ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/from-working-at-small-shop-to-billionaire-boss-lulus-yusuffali-completes-50-year-dream-run-in-uae)).

## What the Gulf built: a high-income state without a matching industrial base

**Growth of the money [C]:** KMS remittances rose from **₹13,652 cr (1998)** to ₹43,288 cr (2008), ₹71,142 cr (2013), ₹85,092 cr (2018) and **₹2,16,893 cr (2023)** ([CDS WP 483](https://cds.edu/wp-content/uploads/WP483.pdf); [KMS 2023](https://iimad.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/KMS-2023-Report.pdf)).

**Macro weight in 2008 [C]:** remittances were
- **1.74x revenue receipts**
- **5.5x central transfers**
- **36x cashew export earnings**

Per-capita income was **₹54,664 with remittances and ₹41,814 without** ([MEI, Zachariah & Irudaya Rajan](https://mei.edu/publication/remittances-kerala-impact-economy/)).

**Consumption [C]:** Kerala's rural monthly per-capita spending was **₹6,611 in HCES 2023–24**, the highest among major states, against ₹4,122 for rural India ([MoSPI](https://www.mospi.gov.in/sites/default/files/press_release/HCES_Press_Note_2023-24_27122024_rev.pdf)). High consumption with modest local production is the signature of a remittance economy.

**Housing [C]**
- Thatched huts gave way to concrete "Gulf houses" ([Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/1064100/exaggerations-silences-and-treasure-keralas-arabian-tale-of-migration-to-the-gulf)).
- The **2011 Census counted 11.89 lakh vacant houses** ([The News Minute, 2017](https://www.thenewsminute.com/kerala/over-3000-people-are-homeless-kerala-while-almost-12-lakh-houses-lie-vacant-69932)).
- The largest single use of remittances in 2023 was **house or shop renovation (15.8%)**, followed by loan repayment (14%) and education (10%) ([KMS 2023](https://iimad.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/KMS-2023-Report.pdf)).

**Uplift of Malabar Muslims [C]:** in 2008, Muslim households received an average of **₹1,19,004**, against **₹37,385** for Hindu households and **₹50,107** for Christian households ([MEI](https://mei.edu/publication/remittances-kerala-impact-economy/)). That is about 3x the Hindu average.

**Social cost [C]**
- **About 1.06 million "Gulf wives" (2008 CDS survey).** Loneliness was their main problem, yet **83% preferred future husbands to work in the Gulf** ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/arts/the-gulf-wives-of-kerala-1.550611)).

**Inequality [C]**
- **Only 12% of households received cash remittances in 2023**, down from 17.1% in 2008 ([The Wire](https://m.thewire.in/article/economy/gulf-dreams-to-global-pathways-how-keralas-migration-economy-transitioned-in-the-last-decade); [MEI](https://mei.edu/publication/remittances-kerala-impact-economy/)).
- Income and consumption inequality rose, and the tax-to-income ratio did not improve ([IJLE 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41027-020-00280-z)).

**Politics [C]**
- The Loka Kerala Sabha (first held in 2018) institutionalised the diaspora ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loka_Kerala_Sabha)).
- In Sept 2026 the UDF government approved it after boycotting it in opposition ([Onmanorama, 21 Sep 2026](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/kerala/2026/09/21/after-boycott-udf-govt-gives-lok-kerala-sabha-go-ahead.html); headline only).

**Culture [C]:** *Aadujeevitham* is based on Najeeb Muhammad, who reached Saudi Arabia in 1992 and was held as a goatherd for about 3.5 years. The novel has been translated into more than 30 languages and the film came out in 2024 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Najeeb_Muhammad)).

**Net assessment:** remittances bought consumption, houses, education and health. They did not buy a productive base. That is why each Gulf shock lands on households rather than on a diversified economy.

## Kerala has survived five Gulf shocks, and each time the state reached only a sliver of returnees

| Episode | Magnitude (dated) | Response | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1990 Kuwait invasion** | **More than 1.7 lakh Indians airlifted on 488 flights**; about **$4 bn** in extra forex burden for India that year ([Onmanorama, 16 May 2020](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/global-indian/2020/05/16/vande-bharat-airlift-kuwait-iraq-india-1990.html)); fed into the 1991 balance-of-payments crisis ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Indian_economic_crisis)) | The Centre evacuated people. **Fewer than 4,000 returnees received help from Kerala** ([Onmanorama](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/global-indian/2020/05/16/vande-bharat-airlift-kuwait-iraq-india-1990.html)) | The hit arrived through the national BoP, and state reintegration was negligible |
| **2008–09 financial crisis** | Emigrant stock still rose from 21.9 lakh (2008) to 22.8 lakh (2011) ([CDS WP 463](https://cds.edu/wp-content/uploads/WP463.pdf)) | None needed at scale | Cyclical shocks only slow growth |
| **Nitaqat / 2013 Saudi amnesty** | More than 1.34 lakh Indians returned, but **only 13,714 Keralites** out of about 4.5 lakh in Saudi Arabia (about 3%) ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/134000-indians-return-from-saudi-arabia-1.1251754)) | NORKA lists and charter flights | Fears of a mass exodus were overstated. Localisation squeezes gradually |
| **2014–16 oil crash, levies** | Emigrants fell from 24.0 lakh (2013/14) to 21.2 lakh (2018) ([CDS WP 483](https://cds.edu/wp-content/uploads/WP483.pdf)); remittance growth slowed after 2015 as rupee depreciation slowed ([IJLE 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41027-020-00280-z)) | No targeted response found | **Structural shocks are the ones that shrink the stock** |
| **COVID 2020–21** | **15 lakh returned; 10.7 lakh had lost jobs** ([Onmanorama](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/kerala/2021/07/06/nri-return-expats-covid-gulf-norka-data.html)); 39% of those who lost jobs reported wage theft ([World Bank](https://blogs.worldbank.org/peoplemove/kerala-return-emigrant-survey-2021-insights-and-way-forward)) | Pravasi Bhadratha (2021); a ₹2,000 cr package proposed to the Centre ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/kerala-to-submit-rs-2000-cr-expatriate-rehabilitation-package-to-centre-1044682.html)) | Deposits rose while jobs vanished. The template for the false-comfort trap |

**The pattern:** the Centre evacuates, the state provides relief, and nobody builds a system to absorb returnees. About 50% of distressed COVID returnees wanted to go back abroad ([World Bank](https://blogs.worldbank.org/peoplemove/kerala-return-emigrant-survey-2021-insights-and-way-forward)). That behaviour is now built into the system: the answer to a Gulf shock has been to re-emigrate.

## Kerala's returnee support is India's most developed and still too small for the job

**The need [C]**
- There were **18 lakh return emigrants in 2023**, up from 12 lakh in 2018, against 21.5 lakh current emigrants ([KMS 2023](https://iimad.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/KMS-2023-Report.pdf)).
- Returnees now almost equal emigrants in number.

**Cumulative outcomes of the main schemes (to Mar 2025).** Source: [Deshabhimani, citing NORKA](https://www.deshabhimani.com/deshabhimani-english-/kerala-news/nrk-remittances-economic-growth-welfare-kerala-76401). Deshabhimani is the CPI(M)'s party paper, and this was a government-achievement piece.

| Scheme | What it does | Output |
|---|---|---|
| **NDPREM** | 15% capital subsidy (up to ₹3 lakh) plus a 3% interest subsidy for 4 years | **10,500+ ventures, ₹106.37 cr** (2016–25). That reaches **about 0.6% of the returnee stock** [Calc] |
| **Santhwana** | Distress relief: medical, death and marriage costs | ₹201.74 cr to 33,458 families |
| **Pravasi Bhadratha** | Post-COVID livelihood loans (up to ₹2 cr) | ₹118.98 cr to 21,800+ people |
| **Pravasi ID card** | Accident cover | 7.53 lakh cards |

**Recent run rate [C]:** NDPREM had **about 1,200 beneficiaries and ₹18 cr** in FY24. According to CPPR, "many returnees [are] unaware the scheme exists" ([CPPR](https://www.cppr.in/archives/kerala-after-2026-gcc-crisis)).

**The pension system [C]**
- The Pravasi Welfare Board had **9.5 lakh members and 94,666 pensioners** (May 2026). The minimum pension is ₹3,000 a month for returnees.
- **Pension applications received after Jan 2026 were unprocessed** as of Jun 2026 ([ETV Bharat, Jun 2026](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/state/kerala-govt-examining-measures-to-streamline-pravasi-welfare-pension-distribution-enn26062403405)). The backlog built up just as crisis returns peaked.

**The 2026 crisis response [C]**
- The NORKA helpdesk logged 381 calls by 1 Mar ([The Week](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2026/03/01/west-asia-conflict-norka-helpdesk-receives-381-calls.html)).
- The revised Budget of **19 Jun 2026**, the first UDF budget under CM V.D. Satheesan, announced:
  - a **Global Pravasi Rehabilitation Scheme**
  - a **Pravasi Investment Company**
  - a **Global Placement Cell**
  - International Skill Centres
  - an expanded Pravasi Chitty

  The report of the budget **gives no allocations or targets** for any of them ([Onmanorama, 19 Jun 2026](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/kerala/2026/06/19/global-pravasi-rehabilitation-scheme-investment-company-nri-budget-announcements.html)).

**Diversification channel [C]:** NORKA's Triple Win programme with Germany recruits nurses and has run at least 6 selection rounds ([NORKA](https://nifl.norkaroots.org/selection-of-nurses-to-germany-under-triplewin-program-6th-edition/)).

**Assessment:** the spending leans toward relief rather than reintegration. Santhwana has paid out about twice what NDPREM has. That fits a returnee pool that is mostly older manual workers, and it means the state has no real mechanism for absorbing them.

## Keralites still go because Kerala pays India's best manual wages and has its worst graduate jobless rates

**The paradox [C]**
- **Kerala's rural construction wage was ₹837.7 a day in 2021–22, against ₹373.3 for India.** Non-farm work paid ₹681.8 against ₹326.6.
- Those wages draw **about 25 lakh interstate migrant workers** into Kerala ([The South First, citing RBI](https://thesouthfirst.com/kerala/rbi-annual-report-kerala-tops-in-daily-wages-of-workers-tamil-nadu-is-third/)).

**Unemployment [C]**
- **Youth unemployment (15–29) was 29%, against 10.2% nationally. Female youth unemployment was 47%** (PLFS Jul 2023–Jun 2024) ([Onmanorama, 10 Oct 2024](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/kerala/2024/10/10/unemployment-highest-kerala-official-figures-considered-last-word.html)).
- The then Finance Minister called the youth figure misleading because the bracket includes students.
- **Graduate unemployment was 19.8%, the highest of any state** (PLFS-based, Aug 2026) ([ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/bharat/plfs-report-1-cr-indian-graduates-unemployed-joblessness-rate-highest-in-kerala-lowest-in-delhi-enn26080605157)).

**Mechanism (inference)**
- There are two labour markets. Manual wages are high enough to import 25 lakh workers, but too low in pay and status for educated Keralites. White-collar jobs at home are scarce.
- The Gulf pays more in absolute terms for the semi-skilled worker. The West is now where the graduate goes.

**The mix is shifting, not shrinking [C]**
- Non-GCC destinations rose from 10.8% of emigrants to 19.5%.
- **Student emigrants nearly doubled to about 2.5 lakh.**
- **71.5% of women emigrants are graduates** ([KMS 2023](https://iimad.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/KMS-2023-Report.pdf); [The Wire](https://m.thewire.in/article/economy/gulf-dreams-to-global-pathways-how-keralas-migration-economy-transitioned-in-the-last-decade)).
- Kerala's share of India's emigration clearances for the GCC fell from **8.16% (2021) to 4.40% (2024)** ([The Wire](https://m.thewire.in/article/economy/gulf-dreams-to-global-pathways-how-keralas-migration-economy-transitioned-in-the-last-decade)).

**New risks in the new channels [C]:** fraudulent education consultancies, and Keralites recruited into the Russian army ([The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/kerala/keralas-migration-paradox-caught-between-promise-and-peril/)).

**Gap:** no sourced comparison of Gulf and Kerala salaries for the same job was found. Do not quote a multiple on camera.

## Counter-thesis: where "Kerala is choking" could be wrong

1. **High oil could refill Gulf hiring.** The post-2014 decline was driven by *falling* crude ([CDS WP 483](https://cds.edu/wp-content/uploads/WP483.pdf)). Brent near $97 is a fiscal windfall for Riyadh and Abu Dhabi. If security returns, reconstruction and project spending could lift demand for labour. (Hypothesis.)
2. **The data so far show resilience.** Private transfers are up about 29% (pending verification) and deposits are rising. Bank MDs say nothing changes without permanent mass returns ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/asia/india-rupee-hits-record-low-but-gulf-remittances-stay-strong-analysts-say)).
3. **Exodus fears have been overstated before.** In the 2013 Nitaqat amnesty, only about 3% of Saudi Keralites returned ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/134000-indians-return-from-saudi-arabia-1.1251754)). A mass evacuation of about 9 million Indians is "practically impossible" ([Enterprise AM, 6 Mar 2026](https://enterpriseam.com/mena-india/2026/03/06/why-a-mass-evacuation-of-9-mn-indians-from-the-gulf-is-practically-impossible/)), so most workers stay unless they are fired.
4. **Dependence is smaller than the headline.** Remittances are about 13–15% of GSDP, not 23% [Calc]. Only 12% of households receive cash remittances.
5. **Kerala is already diversifying.** About 19.5% of emigrants are non-GCC, and they earn more per head.
6. **Rebuttal to all five.** This shock is physical, not fiscal. It hits hospitality, aviation and retail, where Keralites work. The **collapse in NRE inflows (−91%)** is hard evidence that worker savings are already under strain, whatever the headline totals say.

**Net view:** the choke is real but **lagged and concentrated**, in Malabar, Kollam, and among semi-skilled Muslim and Hindu male workers in UAE services. It is not a statewide collapse. The biggest near-term risk is misreading strong early data as safety.

---

## Conclusion

The 2026 war shows that Kerala's vulnerability is less about *how much* money arrives and more about *what* produces it. The inflow depends on a stock of about 17 lakh GCC-based workers [Calc: 21.5 lakh × 80.5%], concentrated in exactly the service sectors a Hormuz war disrupts. It is supported by a household base that already carries 18 lakh returnees and a pension system with a backlog. The rupee and savings repatriation will keep the headline numbers healthy for a few quarters. The signal to watch is the flow tied to workers, meaning NRE inflows and Kerala's share in the next RBI remittance survey, not totals inflated by FCNR arbitrage.

The deeper lesson is institutional. Across 1990, 2013, 2020 and now 2026, Kerala has answered Gulf shocks with evacuation, relief and new scheme names. It has not built the jobs that would make emigration optional. Until graduate unemployment falls from about 20% and returnee programmes reach more than 1% of returnees, every Gulf war will also be a Kerala recession. The state's real fallback will stay what it has been for 50 years: the next flight out.
