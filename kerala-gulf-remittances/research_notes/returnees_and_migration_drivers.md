# Kerala's Returnee Support Arrangements and the Structural Drivers of Gulf Migration (data through Sept 2026)

Context for the writer: Kerala had a change of government in 2026. The first budget of the UDF government (CM V.D. Satheesan) was presented in June 2026, so the older NORKA scheme data (to March 2025) belongs to the previous LDF period. The "2026 GCC crisis" means the US-Israel war against Iran that began in late February 2026.

## Part A: What support exists for returning Gulf migrants, and does it work?

### Takeaway
Kerala has the most developed state-level returnee system in India: NORKA Roots, the Pravasi Welfare Board and a string of schemes. In money terms, though, it is small next to the flows it deals with. Returnee enterprise support (NDPREM) paid out about ₹106 crore over nine years for about 10,500 ventures. In the same period the number of return emigrants rose to about 18 lakh (KMS 2023) and annual remittances reached about ₹2.17 lakh crore. The known weak points are low uptake and low awareness, pension backlogs, and 2026 announcements that came without allocated budgets.

### Cited Findings

**Scale of return migration**
- KMS 2023 counted about 1.8 million (18 lakh) return emigrants, up from 1.2 million in 2018. It counted 2.2 million emigrants in 2023, against 2.1 million in 2018 — [StudyIQ summary of KMS 2023](https://www.studyiq.com/articles/kerala-migration-survey/); primary report: [KMS 2023 Report, IIMAD](https://iimad.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/KMS-2023-Report.pdf)
- About 1.4 million non-resident Keralites returned during COVID-19 (2020–21) — [CPPR, "Kerala After the 2026 GCC Crisis" (2026)](https://www.cppr.in/archives/kerala-after-2026-gcc-crisis)
- In the 2026 crisis, about 984,000 Indian nationals flew home between late February and mid-April 2026, according to India's Ministry of External Affairs. The figure covers workers, students and vulnerable groups and is not broken down by state. About 9 million Indians were working in the Gulf in early 2026 — [Hellenic Shipping News / DW report (Apr 2026)](https://www.hellenicshippingnews.com/indians-forced-to-return-home-as-iran-war-rocks-gulf-economy/)
- The same report describes layoffs in Gulf hospitality, such as a Dubai hotel worker from Kerala laid off in April 2026 after occupancy fell. Cargo costs from Dubai to Kochi rose about 30% — [Hellenic Shipping News](https://www.hellenicshippingnews.com/indians-forced-to-return-home-as-iran-war-rocks-gulf-economy/)

**NORKA Roots schemes: cumulative outcomes (Deshabhimani, citing NORKA data to March 2025)**
Caveat: Deshabhimani is the CPI(M) party paper, and this was a government-achievement piece written under the previous LDF government.
- **NDPREM** (NORKA Department Project for Return Emigrants): 10,500+ ventures supported, ₹106.37 crore in total assistance, April 2016 to March 2025. It offers a capital subsidy of up to ₹3 lakh per venture (15%) and a 3% interest subsidy for 4 years, with loans routed through partner banks — [Deshabhimani](https://www.deshabhimani.com/deshabhimani-english-/kerala-news/nrk-remittances-economic-growth-welfare-kerala-76401); scheme page: [NORKA NDPREM](https://norkaroots.org/ndprem)
- NORKA renewed its NDPREM/NRK business-loan agreement with SBI in July 2025 — [UAE Vartha (3 Jul 2025)](https://english.uaevartha.com/2025/07/03/norka-roots-renews-agreement-with-sbi-for-nrk-business-loan-scheme/)
- **Santhwana** (distress relief for returnees and families, covering medical, death and marriage costs): ₹201.74 crore paid to 33,458 families since 2016 — [Deshabhimani](https://www.deshabhimani.com/deshabhimani-english-/kerala-news/nrk-remittances-economic-growth-welfare-kerala-76401)
- **Pravasi Bhadratha** (post-COVID livelihood scheme, launched 2021): ₹118.98 crore to 21,800+ beneficiaries. It has three tiers: Pearl (up to ₹2 lakh), Micro (up to ₹5 lakh with a ₹1 lakh subsidy) and Mega (up to ₹2 crore) — [Deshabhimani](https://www.deshabhimani.com/deshabhimani-english-/kerala-news/nrk-remittances-economic-growth-welfare-kerala-76401)
- **NORKA Pravasi ID card**: 7.53 lakh cards issued 2016 to March 2025, covering accidental death (₹4 lakh) and disability (₹2 lakh) — [Deshabhimani](https://www.deshabhimani.com/deshabhimani-english-/kerala-news/nrk-remittances-economic-growth-welfare-kerala-76401)
- **Pravasi Raksha insurance** (from 2021): 47,749 enrolled, premium ₹649 a year — same source
- **NORKA Business Facilitation Centre** (from 2021): 31 training programmes, 501 business units, ₹208 crore combined investment — same source
- **Directors' Scholarship**: 2,347 students, ₹3.8 crore since 2019. **Emergency ambulance**: 1,846 users since 2021 — same source
- A news headline says the NORKA Care health/accident insurance plan will cover expat returnees and their families. Only the headline was accessible; no numbers were found — [Gulf Today via Magzter](https://www.magzter.com/stories/newspaper/Gulf-Today/KERALAS-NORKA-CARE-PLAN-TO-INCLUDE-EXPAT-RETURNEES-KIN)

**Kerala Pravasi Welfare Board (welfare fund and pension)**
- As of May 2026 it had 9.5 lakh members and 94,666 active pensioners. The minimum monthly pension is ₹3,500 for expatriates abroad and ₹3,000 for returnees and those working in other Indian states. Membership is open to ages 18–60 — [ETV Bharat (Jun 2026)](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/state/kerala-govt-examining-measures-to-streamline-pravasi-welfare-pension-distribution-enn26062403405)
- There is a processing backlog: "a large number of pension applications received by the Kerala Pravasi Welfare Board up to January 2026 were yet to be processed." No action had been taken on applications received after January 2026, and the new government was "examining measures to streamline" distribution — [ETV Bharat (Jun 2026)](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/state/kerala-govt-examining-measures-to-streamline-pravasi-welfare-pension-distribution-enn26062403405)
- Scale check: 9.5 lakh members is less than half of the 22 lakh emigrants, and far fewer than emigrants plus 18 lakh returnees. This is my comparison of the figures above, not a sourced statistic.

**Critiques of effectiveness**
- In FY 2023-24, NDPREM had only about 1,200 beneficiaries and spent ₹18 crore. The source's line is that "many returnees [are] unaware the scheme exists" (CPPR citing Kumar, 2025) — [CPPR (2026)](https://www.cppr.in/archives/kerala-after-2026-gcc-crisis)
- CPPR recommends decentralising Pravasi Welfare offices into high-migration taluks, scaling NDPREM through awareness campaigns, upskilling semi-skilled returnees, and teaching young people about migration — [CPPR](https://www.cppr.in/archives/kerala-after-2026-gcc-crisis)
- Fiscal limits: Kerala's aggregate debt is about ₹5.07 lakh crore, and CPPR puts the 2026-27 revenue shortfall at about ₹20,500 crore — [CPPR](https://www.cppr.in/archives/kerala-after-2026-gcc-crisis)
- An older independent assessment exists: CDS / S. Irudaya Rajan's assessment of NORKA Roots (Feb 2012) — [Migrant Forum Asia PDF](https://mfasia.org/migrantforumasia/wp-content/uploads/2012/02/rajan_cds_norka-reportfeb2012.pdf). I did not review its contents.

**2026 crisis response and new measures**
- When the conflict broke out, NORKA set up a helpdesk that received 381 calls by 1 March 2026 — [The Week (1 Mar 2026)](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2026/03/01/west-asia-conflict-norka-helpdesk-receives-381-calls.html)
- NORKA also formed Gulf coordination and support teams for Malayalis — [Onmanorama (2 Mar 2026)](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/kerala/2026/03/02/norka-roots-gulf-support-centres.html); [Deshabhimani](https://www.deshabhimani.com/deshabhimani-english-/kerala-news/norka-support-gulf-malayalis-middle-east-tensions-78225)
- The Kerala Budget 2026-27 (revised, first UDF budget, 19 Jun 2026) announced:
  - a **Global Pravasi Rehabilitation Scheme**, offering "sustainable livelihood models, priority financial assistance and skill re-certification"
  - a **Pravasi Investment Company** to channel diaspora capital into infrastructure
  - a restructured and expanded **Pravasi Dividend Scheme**
  - a **Global Placement Cell** for "safe, legal and organised migration channels"
  - enhanced distress relief and legal aid
  - a single-window upgrade of the NORKA Business Facilitation Centre
  - an expanded KSFE Pravasi Chitty
  - International Skill Training Centres

  The article reports **no specific allocations or targets** for any of these — [Onmanorama (19 Jun 2026)](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/kerala/2026/06/19/global-pravasi-rehabilitation-scheme-investment-company-nri-budget-announcements.html)
- Precedent: in 2020 (COVID), Kerala prepared a ₹2,000 crore expatriate rehabilitation package to submit to the Centre — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/kerala-to-submit-rs-2000-cr-expatriate-rehabilitation-package-to-centre-1044682.html)

**Europe recruitment (a channel away from the Gulf)**
- NORKA runs the Triple Win programme with Germany's GIZ and the Federal Employment Agency to recruit nurses, and it has reached at least a 6th selection round — [NIFL/NORKA 6th edition notice](https://nifl.norkaroots.org/selection-of-nurses-to-germany-under-triplewin-program-6th-edition/); [GIZ](https://www.giz.de/en/newsroom/stories/triple-win-nurses-germany)

### Inferences
- NDPREM's run rate (about 1,200 ventures and ₹18 crore a year in FY24; about 10,500 ventures over 9 years) would reach well under 1% of the 18 lakh return-emigrant stock. At that scale the scheme is symbolic, not a system-level absorber.
- The schemes lean toward relief and welfare: Santhwana at about ₹202 crore is bigger than NDPREM at about ₹106 crore, and the pension adds more. That fits a returnee population that is largely older, manual workers (76.9% in manual roles, per CPPR). Entrepreneurship schemes suit only a minority of them.
- The 2026 budget renames and expands the architecture (rehabilitation, investment company, placement cell), but announcing schemes without allocations looks like a pattern. Future budget documents or outcomes should be checked before anyone treats these as real capacity.
- The pension backlog (applications unprocessed since January 2026) arrived just as crisis returns peaked. That points to an administrative capacity problem, not only a design one.

## Part B: Why Keralites still migrate to the Gulf for work

### Takeaway
Kerala has the highest wages for manual work in India, and it still has the highest youth and graduate unemployment. Educated workers, especially women, cannot find jobs at home that match their education and status. Meanwhile about 25 lakh interstate migrant workers fill Kerala's high-wage manual jobs. The Gulf, and increasingly the UK, Canada and Europe, supplies the "desired" jobs the local economy does not. Destinations are diversifying, and migration now runs through students as well as workers.

### Cited Findings

**Unemployment**
- PLFS July 2023–June 2024: Kerala's youth unemployment (ages 15–29) was 29%, against a 10.2% national average. Female youth unemployment was 47%, against 11% nationally — [Onmanorama (10 Oct 2024)](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/kerala/2024/10/10/unemployment-highest-kerala-official-figures-considered-last-word.html)
- Other reports put Kerala's youth unemployment at 29.9% in 2023-24 PLFS reporting — [News Karnataka (26 Sep 2024)](https://newskarnataka.com/education/keralas-youth-unemployment-crisis-reaches-record-high-of-29-9-plfs-report/26092024/)
- The then Finance Minister K.N. Balagopal called the PLFS youth figures "pointless" because the 15–29 bracket includes students. He cited Kerala PSC issuing 34,110 recruitment advices in 2023 — [Onmanorama](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/kerala/2024/10/10/unemployment-highest-kerala-official-figures-considered-last-word.html)
- A PLFS-based report (Aug 2026) says Kerala has the highest graduate unemployment among states at 19.8%, ahead of Odisha (18.9%), Bihar (16.6%) and Tamil Nadu (16.3%) — [ETV Bharat (6 Aug 2026)](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/bharat/plfs-report-1-cr-indian-graduates-unemployed-joblessness-rate-highest-in-kerala-lowest-in-delhi-enn26080605157)
  - **Caveat:** the same article gives a "national average of 22.7%" for graduates, which contradicts Kerala being highest. The national figure is probably for a different group, such as young graduates. Check against the PLFS source before using.
- Historical: Kerala's youth unemployment was 40.5% in Jan–Mar 2020, against 21% nationally. Among the educated, about 25% of postgraduates and 20% of graduates were unemployed — [Wikipedia, Unemployment in Kerala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment_in_Kerala) (secondary source; use as background only)
- The PLFS calendar-year 2025 microdata is released — [MoSPI microdata catalog](https://microdata.gov.in/NADA/index.php/catalog/284). I did not extract Kerala's 2025 annual youth rate from it.

**The wage paradox and inbound migrant workers**
- RBI data for 2021-22 shows Kerala's rural daily wages at:

  | Rural work (2021-22) | Kerala | India |
  |---|---|---|
  | Non-agricultural | ₹681.8 | ₹326.6 |
  | Construction | ₹837.7 | ₹373.3 |
  | Agricultural | ₹726.8 | ₹323.3 |

  These are the highest of any state. About 25 lakh migrant workers live in Kerala, drawn by the high wages — [The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/kerala/rbi-annual-report-kerala-tops-in-daily-wages-of-workers-tamil-nadu-is-third/)
- A later RBI handbook figure puts Kerala's rural construction wage at ₹893.6/day against ₹417.3 nationally (probably FY2023-24). This comes via a social media post, so verify it against the RBI table — [X/@IndianTechGuide](https://x.com/IndianTechGuide/status/1901611987425226923?lang=en); [RBI Table 93](https://rbidocs.rbi.org.in/rdocs/Publications/PDFs/T_933F1F9BB711CF41739B4C3BD535855E2C.PDF)
- Causes commonly cited:
  - high reservation wages among educated workers
  - skill mismatch
  - inbound migrant labour taking low-wage manual jobs
  - a weak manufacturing sector
  - an economy that is about 63% services and about 8% agriculture by GSVA

  Source: [Wikipedia, Unemployment in Kerala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment_in_Kerala) (secondary; the GSVA shares are undated there)

**Who migrates and where to (KMS 2023)**
- Most emigrants are manual labour migrants: 76.9% work in manual roles. Top occupations are sales (12.9%), transport (11.0%) and construction (2.7%) — [CPPR](https://www.cppr.in/archives/kerala-after-2026-gcc-crisis)
- The non-GCC share of destinations rose from 10.8% (2018) to 19.5% (2023), so the Gulf share fell from about 89% to about 80.5% — [StudyIQ/KMS 2023](https://www.studyiq.com/articles/kerala-migration-survey/)
  - Media in 2026 still say "nearly 90%" — [American Bazaar (Apr 2026)](https://americanbazaaronline.com/2026/04/05/u-s-israel-war-against-iran-shakes-gulf-kerala-feels-shock-478248/). That is outdated relative to KMS 2023.
- Students abroad reached 2.5 lakh in 2023 (11.3% of emigrants), up from 1.3 lakh in 2018. Over 80% leave after graduating. Ernakulam (43,990), Thrissur (35,873) and Kottayam (35,382) send the most. Emigration declined in 9 of 14 districts between 2018 and 2023 — [The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/kerala/keralas-migration-paradox-caught-between-promise-and-peril/); [StudyIQ](https://www.studyiq.com/articles/kerala-migration-survey/)
- Women were 19.1% of emigrants in 2023, up from 15.8% in 2018, and 71.5% of female emigrants are graduates. By religion, 41.9% of emigrants are Muslim and 22.3% Christian — [StudyIQ/KMS 2023](https://www.studyiq.com/articles/kerala-migration-survey/)
  - **Caveat:** the Hindu share appears as "2%" in this summary, which is almost certainly a transcription error. Check the KMS report.

**Remittance dependence (the pull factor, seen from home)**
- KMS 2023 remittances were ₹2,16,893 crore, up from ₹85,092 crore in 2018 (+154.9%). That is ₹61,118 per capita and ₹2.24 lakh per remittance-receiving household — [StudyIQ/KMS 2023](https://www.studyiq.com/articles/kerala-migration-survey/)
- Kerala's share of India's GCC-origin remittances rose from 10.2% (2020-21) to 19.7% (2023-24). About 80% of Kerala's diaspora inflows come from the GCC. NRI deposits in Kerala banks exceed ₹2.93 lakh crore. A 20% cut in remittances is projected from the 2026 crisis — [CPPR](https://www.cppr.in/archives/kerala-after-2026-gcc-crisis); [American Bazaar](https://americanbazaaronline.com/2026/04/05/u-s-israel-war-against-iran-shakes-gulf-kerala-feels-shock-478248/)
- About one in five Indian Gulf workers is Malayali, and over 1 million Keralites live in the UAE — [American Bazaar (Apr 2026)](https://americanbazaaronline.com/2026/04/05/u-s-israel-war-against-iran-shakes-gulf-kerala-feels-shock-478248/)
- Risks of the new migration channels: fraudulent education consultancies, Keralites recruited into the Russian army, and people jailed abroad through agent fraud. The response was Operation Shubha Yatra and a task force — [The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/kerala/keralas-migration-paradox-caught-between-promise-and-peril/)

### Inferences
- The "paradox" is really two separate labour markets:
  - Kerala's manual wages (about 2x the national average) are high enough to draw about 25 lakh interstate workers.
  - The same wages do not pay what educated Keralites expect, in money or in status, while Gulf pay for similar work is higher in absolute terms.
  - Meanwhile, the educated, white-collar jobs graduates want are scarce at home, which is why graduate and female youth unemployment is the highest in India.
- Emigration is changing more than shrinking. The emigrant stock is roughly flat (2.1M to 2.2M), but students have nearly doubled and non-GCC destinations have nearly doubled. Educated and female migrants are moving toward the West, while the Gulf remains the main destination for manual and semi-skilled workers.
- Rising return migration (1.2M to 1.8M) alongside flat emigration suggests Gulf migration is becoming a lifecycle event: people go, return older, and depend on welfare. That links Part B to Part A's welfare-heavy scheme mix.
