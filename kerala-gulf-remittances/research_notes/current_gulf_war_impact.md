## 1. What is "the current Gulf war"? Parties, timeline, geography, GCC states hit

### Takeaway
The "current Gulf war" is the 2026 Iran war, begun 28 Feb 2026 when the US and Israel struck Iran (killing Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei); Iran retaliated against Israel and US-host GCC states (UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman) and choked the Strait of Hormuz. An April ceasefire and a June 17 "Islamabad Memorandum" briefly lifted the blockade, but the truce collapsed on 8 July 2026; as of late September 2026 the war is in a live, maritime-centred phase with daily tanker/ship strikes in and around Hormuz, repeated hits on Saudi facilities, and failed diplomacy (US walkout at UNGA, ~23 Sept).

### Cited Findings
- 28 Feb 2026: US and Israel launched Operation "Epic Fury"/"Roaring Lion" against Iran, killing Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei and other officials; Iran responded with "Operation True Promise IV" missile/drone salvos (28 Feb–2 Mar) — [Wikipedia: 2026 Iran war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war); overview also at [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/event/2026-Iran-war)
- Wikipedia lists the coalition as US, Israel, Saudi Arabia, UAE, Kuwait, Bahrain vs. Iran plus Hezbollah, Houthis, Iraqi militias/PMF (note: GCC "belligerent" status is as listed by Wikipedia; treat with caution — they were primarily targets of Iranian strikes on US-linked assets) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war)
- Milestones (per Wikipedia): 2 Mar US Embassy Kuwait struck and Hormuz shipping halted; 3 Mar Iranian frigate IRIS Dena sunk; 8 Mar Mojtaba Khamenei designated Supreme Leader; 18 Mar South Pars struck and Qatar LNG (Ras Laffan) targeted in retaliation; 28 Mar Houthis join; 8 Apr two-week ceasefire; 12–13 Apr Islamabad talks fail; 17 Jun Islamabad Memorandum signed, "dual blockade" lifted; 28 Jun US–Iran agree to stop exchanges; 8 Jul ceasefire collapsed after Iranian attacks on commercial vessels — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war); see also [2026 Iran war ceasefire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war_ceasefire), [2026 US naval blockade of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_naval_blockade_of_Iran)

This means sustained, not one-off, stress on GCC economies.

## 2. Impact on GCC economies: oil, Hormuz, airspace, flights to Kerala, jobs

### Takeaway
Brent spiked from ~$80 to $120–126 in March 2026, eased toward pre-war levels during the June truce, then re-surged after 8 July; it was ~$97–99 in early September 2026. Hormuz traffic remains a fraction of normal. Gulf aviation is heavily disrupted (major foreign carriers suspended into late Oct 2026–Mar 2027; Kuwait airspace repeatedly closed), and Kerala–Gulf routes face chronic cancellations. UAE labour markets are weakening: one in four UAE employers planned Q3 2026 layoffs, concentrated in aviation, tourism, hospitality, construction, catering.

Net effect on Gulf jobs is negative, concentrated in the UAE/Dubai services economy and Kuwait/Bahrain/Qatar.

## 3. Indian government actions: evacuation, advisories, number of Indians in the region

### Takeaway
India did not mount a mass evacuation from the GCC; instead it relied on commercial flights — 12.12 lakh (1.21 million) passengers flew from the Gulf to India between 28 Feb and 23 Apr 2026. Targeted evacuations were from Iran (2,428 moved via Armenia/Azerbaijan by 23 Apr). Indian casualties have been mainly seafarers in Hormuz plus at least one civilian in Abu Dhabi.

## 4. Remittance data during the conflict (RBI, exchange houses, rupee, Kerala NRI deposits)

### Takeaway
Hard data so far show remittances holding up or even rising in 2026 — driven by precautionary "bring money home" transfers and a record-weak rupee — rather than collapsing. RBI BoP data (via a secondary summary) show private transfers of $42.9 bn in Apr–Jun 2026 vs $33.2 bn a year earlier; Kerala NRI deposits reached ₹3.24 lakh crore at 31 Mar 2026. Early-war exchange-house reports of a 4–5% dip in transaction counts from the Gulf coexist with larger average ticket sizes.

### Inferences
- The observed 2026 pattern matches the classic "front-loading" response: migrants repatriate savings when insecure, and a weak rupee raises rupee-value receipts. This inflates near-term data and can mask a later decline once job losses mature (the lag seen in 2020–21).
- The ~29% YoY jump in Q1 FY27 private transfers is very large and should be verified; if accurate it reflects precautionary/savings repatriation and possibly RBI-incentivised FCNR flows, not higher earnings.

## 6. Analyst/economist estimates and scenarios; oil and rupee effects on India

### Takeaway
The benchmark projection is a ~20% drop in Kerala remittances if the war persists (S. Irudaya Rajan, IIMAD; Divya Balan, FLAME; echoed by CPPR in Aug 2026), with downside beyond 20% on escalation. These are projections; realised data so far show resilience. At national level, oil at ~$97–126 widens India's trade deficit (Q1 FY27 merchandise deficit $86.1 bn) and CAD, while the weak rupee (~95–97/$) inflates rupee-value remittances.

### Cited Findings
- S. ### Inferences
- Two offsetting channels: (a) volume risk — GCC layoffs, returns, disrupted travel reduce the migrant stock and earnings; (b) price/behaviour cushion — rupee depreciation (roughly 5–8% YTD) and precautionary repatriation raise near-term rupee inflows. Net: near-term data resilient; downside likely lagged 2–4 quarters if the maritime war and UAE services slump persist.
- Higher oil prices historically support GCC hiring over the medium term (fiscal windfall), which could partly offset once security normalises — a key difference from the 2015–16 oil-crash episode.
