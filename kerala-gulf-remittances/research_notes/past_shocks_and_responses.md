# Past Gulf Remittance/Migration Shocks to Kerala and State/Centre Responses (1990–2025)

Research note for historical context on the 2026 Gulf war shock. Tool budget was limited to about 17 calls, so several episodes (1997–98, 2003, 2009 Dubai, 2017 Qatar blockade and Saudi levy) have thin or no sourced numbers. They are listed under Gaps and are not filled in from memory.

## 1. 1990–91 Iraq invasion of Kuwait / Gulf War: magnitude and response

### Takeaway
India's largest civilian evacuation moved about 1.5–1.7 lakh Indians out of Kuwait/Iraq in about two months (Aug–Oct 1990). The shock added roughly $4 bn to India's forex burden in a single year, and together with the oil price spike it helped trigger the 1991 BoP crisis. Kerala's rehabilitation of returnees was minimal: fewer than 4,000 returnees got state help.

### Cited Findings
- Over 1,70,000 Indians were airlifted from Kuwait in 59 days using 488 flights. Air India set a Guinness World Record for the most civilians evacuated by a civil airline. Most evacuees went overland from Kuwait/Baghdad to Amman before flying out. — [Onmanorama, 16 May 2020](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/global-indian/2020/05/16/vande-bharat-airlift-kuwait-iraq-india-1990.html)
- The same article also says "over 1,50,000 Indian nationals were evacuated" within about 2.5 months, so the two figures don't agree internally. It puts the extra forex burden on India that year at $4 bn, from lost exports, halted remittances, repatriation costs and higher oil prices. 9,775 tonnes of food and medicine went on MV Vishva Siddhi, which arrived on 26 Sep 1990. — [Onmanorama](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/global-indian/2020/05/16/vande-bharat-airlift-kuwait-iraq-india-1990.html)
- On Kerala's response, "less than 4,000 returnees only have benefited from assistance from Kerala". Many lacked the resources and skills to rebuild their lives. — [Onmanorama](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/global-indian/2020/05/16/vande-bharat-airlift-kuwait-iraq-india-1990.html)
- The Gulf War pushed up India's oil import bill, exports slumped and credit dried up. Forex reserves were about $1.2 bn in Jan 1991 and had halved by June, covering about 3 weeks of imports. Between 21 and 31 May 1991, RBI airlifted 47 t of gold to the Bank of England and sent 20 t to UBS to raise $600 mn. — [Wikipedia: 1991 Indian economic crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Indian_economic_crisis) (secondary source; its page doesn't discuss remittances directly)
- Academic work on the episode exists (Saith 1992, "Absorbing External Shocks: The Gulf Crisis, International Migration Linkages and the Indian Economy, 1990", *Development and Change* 23(1):101–146). I found the reference but did not read the text. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/devchg/v23y1992i1p101-146.html); cited in [Rajan & Zachariah, IJLE 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41027-020-00280-z)

### Inferences
- 1990 is the closest precedent to a war-driven shock: a sudden, forced, mass physical evacuation, with the Centre's diplomacy and logistics doing the heavy lifting. The state played almost no role in reintegration.
- At the macro level, the remittance hit mattered mainly through India's BoP (oil and forex), not through a measured collapse in Kerala's consumption.

### Gaps
- I found no source giving the Keralite share of the 1.5–1.7 lakh evacuees, although Keralites are widely described as the largest group. Saith (1992) likely has this.
- I found no source quantifying the fall in remittances to Kerala in 1990–91.
- I did not find the terms of any specific Kerala government package in 1990–91 beyond the "<4,000 assisted" figure.

## 2. The long-run KMS series: how the migration stock responded to 1998, 2008–09, Nitaqat and the 2014–16 oil crash

### Takeaway
The Kerala Migration Survey (CDS/IIMAD) shows emigrants rising from 13.6 lakh (1998) to a peak of 24.0 lakh (2013/14), then falling to 22.4 lakh (2016) and 21.2 lakh (2018). The 2008–09 crisis barely dented the stock, which still grew from 21.9 lakh in 2008 to 22.8 lakh in 2011. The decline after 2014 was structural: oil prices, Nitaqat and family levies, a demographic squeeze and wage convergence.

### Cited Findings
- KMS emigrants (EMI) and return emigrants (REM), in lakh: 1998 EMI 13.6, REM 7.4; 2003 EMI 18.4, REM 8.9; 2008 EMI 21.9, REM 11.6; 2011 EMI 22.8, REM 11.5; 2014 EMI 24.0, REM 12.5. — [CDS Working Paper 463 (Zachariah & Rajan, KMS 2014)](https://cds.edu/wp-content/uploads/WP463.pdf)
- KMS 2018 puts EMI at about 21.2 lakh and REM at about 12.9 lakh. — [CDS Working Paper 483, "New Evidences from KMS 2018"](https://cds.edu/wp-content/uploads/WP483.pdf); [IIMAD summary](https://iimad.org/publications/new-evidences-from-the-kerala-migration-survey-2018/). Note: my automated extraction of WP483 garbled the EMI/REM columns. The 2016 (22.4 lakh) and 2018 (21.2 lakh) values should be confirmed against WP483 Table 1 before publication.
- Total remittances to Kerala (KMS, ₹ crore): 1998: 13,652; 2003: 18,465; 2008: 43,288; 2011: 49,695; 2013/14: 71,142; 2018: 85,092. Remittances kept rising in nominal terms through every episode. — [CDS WP483](https://cds.edu/wp-content/uploads/WP483.pdf)
- Household-level remittances (KMS, ₹ crore): 1998: 3,530; 2003: 7,965; 2008: 12,511; 2011: 15,129; 2014: 24,374. — [CDS WP463](https://cds.edu/wp-content/uploads/WP463.pdf)
- Remittances as a share of NSDP rose to 36.3% in 2014 and fell to 19.3% by 2018 (WP483). Rajan & Zachariah (2020) give a different series: 23.5% of NSDP by 2013–14, falling to 13.3%. The two use different denominators and methods, so these figures should not be mixed. — [CDS WP483](https://cds.edu/wp-content/uploads/WP483.pdf); [Rajan & Zachariah, IJLE 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41027-020-00280-z)
- Rajan & Zachariah say "lower growth in remittances since 2015 is related to a decline in emigration as well as a lower rate of depreciation of the Indian rupee". — [IJLE 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41027-020-00280-z)
- Drivers of the decline listed in KMS 2018: crude prices falling since about 2014, which slowed GCC growth; nationalisation policies ("Nitaqat and family taxes impose a huge burden on these emigrants"); the 15–29 age cohort shrinking from 52.7% of the labour force (1981) to 36.9% (2011); domestic wages rising while unskilled Gulf wages fell; and educated workers heading to the West instead. The paper frames this as a "migration hump". — [CDS WP483](https://cds.edu/wp-content/uploads/WP483.pdf)
- KMS 2018 reasons for return: job loss or layoff 29.4%, health about 14%, family about 12%. — [CDS WP483](https://cds.edu/wp-content/uploads/WP483.pdf)
- KMS 2014 treats the 2008 recession as a "relatively short-term problem"; emigration increased after 2008. It attributes to Nitaqat a fall in Muslims' share of emigrants from 44.3% (2011) to 41.3% (2014), with Saudi Arabia's share of Kerala emigrants down about 3 percentage points. It warned that a steep oil price fall could have a "long-term effect" because about 90% of Kerala emigration goes to the GCC. — [CDS WP463](https://cds.edu/wp-content/uploads/WP463.pdf)

### Inferences
- Across episodes, cyclical shocks (1998, 2008–09) showed up only as slower growth in the stock. The measurable decline came from structural or policy shocks (Nitaqat 2011+, the oil crash 2014–16, the 2017 levies). A war shock that permanently changes GCC hiring would look more like the post-2014 pattern than like 2008.
- The return emigrant stock (about 12–13 lakh by 2014–18) was already comparable to half the emigrant stock before COVID, so reintegration was a large, chronic problem well before 2020.

### Gaps
- 1997–98 Asian crisis / 1998 oil crash: I found no Kerala-specific figures on remittance or returnee changes. KMS 1998 is the first survey baseline, so no pre-crisis comparison exists in KMS.
- 2009 Dubai World: no Kerala returnee numbers sourced.
- 2014–16 oil crash: no direct count of returnees or of the change in GCC hiring (e.g. eMigrate/ECR clearances) was sourced.
- 2017 Saudi dependent fee/expat levy: I found no quantified Kerala impact.

## 3. Saudi Nitaqat (2011–13), the 2013 amnesty and later localisation drives

### Takeaway
Media fears of a mass Nitaqat exodus in 2013 turned out to be overstated. About 1.34 lakh Indians returned from Saudi Arabia during the 2013 amnesty, but only about 13,700 had returned to Kerala at the time of one Gulf News report, out of roughly 4.5 lakh Keralites in Saudi Arabia. The state's response consisted of NORKA lists and chartered flights.

### Cited Findings
- More than 1,34,000 Indian workers returned from Saudi Arabia. Kerala's diaspora survey counted 4,50,229 Keralites in Saudi Arabia, and only 13,714 had returned to Kerala by the reporting date. Kerala planned charter flights, with NORKA-Roots regional committees listing people who needed to be brought back. The amnesty began on 4 April. — [Gulf News, "134,000 Indians return from Saudi Arabia"](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/134000-indians-return-from-saudi-arabia-1.1251754). Caution: the automated extraction gave the report date as "September 2018", but the content (Nitaqat amnesty starting 4 April) and URL numbering point to 2013. Verify the date.
- Nitaqat reserved about 10% of jobs for Saudi nationals in all establishments. — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/134000-indians-return-from-saudi-arabia-1.1251754)
- UAE amnesty 2018: Kerala appointed contact persons and offered free air tickets home for Keralites using the amnesty. — [Gulf News, 2018](https://gulfnews.com/news/uae/government/keralites-in-uae-availing-amnesty-scheme-to-fly-home-for-free-1.2259263); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/news/asia/india/uae-amnesty-2018-kerala-appoints-contact-persons-for-free-air-tickets-1.2263097)

### Inferences
- The pattern in localisation episodes is a gradual squeeze (hiring, levies, sector bans) rather than an abrupt exodus. That explains why KMS picks up a decline in the stock only in 2016–18.

### Gaps
- No sourced numbers on Kuwaitisation, Omanisation or Emiratisation quotas and their Kerala impact.
- No sourced number of Kerala returnees attributable to the 2017 Saudi dependent fee (SAR 100/month per dependent from July 2017, rising yearly; not verified here).

## 4. COVID-19 (2020–21): the largest reverse flow, and remittance resilience

### Takeaway
About 14.3–15 lakh Non-Resident Keralites returned between May 2020 and mid-2021, and over 10.7 lakh of them cited job loss. Yet Kerala's NRI bank deposits rose 14% in FY21, and most surveyed returnees said their remittances had increased. Kerala's share of India's remittances halved in the RBI survey (19% in 2016–17 to 10.2% in 2020–21), then recovered to about 19.7% of GCC-origin remittances by 2023–24.

### Cited Findings
- NORKA data: 15,01,326 NRIs returned to Kerala between May 2020 and 3 July 2021. By country: UAE 8,90,485; Saudi Arabia 1,73,561; Qatar 1,47,917; Oman 1,36,445; Kuwait 52,032; Bahrain 44,246; non-Gulf 56,640. Over 10.73 lakh cited job loss, and 2,96,240 had expired visas. — [Onmanorama, 6 Jul 2021](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/kerala/2021/07/06/nri-return-expats-covid-gulf-norka-data.html)
- Other estimates: about 14.7 lakh returnees, 59% from the UAE (NORKA, cited by [Onmanorama, 18 Jul 2022](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/business/2022/07/18/nri-remittance-share-india.html)); about 14.3 lakh between May 2020 and April 2021 ([World Bank People Move blog, Kerala Return Emigrant Survey 2021](https://blogs.worldbank.org/peoplemove/kerala-return-emigrant-survey-2021-insights-and-way-forward)); and "1.4 million" in 2020–21 ([CPPR, Aug 2026](https://www.cppr.in/archives/kerala-after-2026-gcc-crisis)).
- Kerala Return Emigrant Survey 2021 (n=1,985, fieldwork May–Dec 2020): 47% had lost jobs, and 39% of those reported wage theft or unpaid dues. 75% had been sending remittances. Monthly remittances increased after COVID for over 70% of the sample. About 50% of distressed returnees wanted to re-emigrate, while 32% sought work in Kerala or retirement. Only 3% received any guidance on wage theft. — [World Bank blog](https://blogs.worldbank.org/peoplemove/kerala-return-emigrant-survey-2021-insights-and-way-forward)
- NRI deposits in Kerala rose from ₹1,99,781 cr (FY20) to ₹2,27,430 cr (FY21), up 14% or ₹27,649 cr. An SLBC official attributed this to returnees who lost jobs or retired depositing their entire savings in NRE accounts for tax reasons. — [Business Today, 29 Jun 2021](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/economy-politics/story/nri-bank-deposits-to-kerala-rise-14-at-rs-227-lakh-crore-in-fy21-299939-2021-06-29)
- RBI remittance survey: Kerala's share of India's inward remittances fell from 19% (2016–17) to 10.2% (2020–21), while Maharashtra's rose from 16.7% to 35.2%. The GCC share of India's remittances fell from about 50% to 30%, the UAE's from 26.6% to 18%, and the US led with 22.9%. — [Onmanorama, 18 Jul 2022](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/business/2022/07/18/nri-remittance-share-india.html)
- Kerala's share recovered to 19.7% of India's GCC remittances in 2023–24. Kerala receives about ₹2.16 lakh cr a year from its diaspora, about 80% of whom live in the GCC. — [CPPR, 6 Aug 2026](https://www.cppr.in/archives/kerala-after-2026-gcc-crisis); see also [Onmanorama, 20 Mar 2025](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/kerala/2025/03/20/kerala-remittance-increase-post-pandemic-rbi-report.html)
- World Bank: 2020 remittances to India from the UAE fell about 17% because of job losses and repatriation. — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/banking/2020-remittances-to-india-from-uae-in-steep-17-drop-on-job-losses-repatriation-world-bank-1.1620979981976) (headline only; not fetched)
- State schemes: Santhwana is a distress relief fund for returnees with at least 2 years abroad and family income below ₹1 lakh/yr. NDPREM gives a 15% capital subsidy on business loans but has a high default rate and long sanction delays. The NRK Welfare Board "does not encourage or help return migrants to find suitable job opportunity". — [Azim Premji University policy brief](https://azimpremjiuniversity.edu.in/response-to-covid-19-upc-series/reintegration-rehabilitation-policies-for-emigrants-in-kerala-strategies-for-a-post-covid-world)
- NDPREM had only about 1,200 beneficiaries and ₹18 cr spent in FY2023–24, limited by poor awareness. — [CPPR, Aug 2026](https://www.cppr.in/archives/kerala-after-2026-gcc-crisis)
- Returnee expatriates were still waiting for promised support in 2025–26. — [The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/kerala/kerala-cms-gulf-outreach-meets-a-stark-reminder-returnee-expatriates-still-await-promised-support/) (headline only; not fetched)

### Inferences
- The nearly halved RBI share in 2020–21 partly reflects RBI's survey methodology (bank location of receipt, which boosts Maharashtra and Mumbai). It should not be read as Kerala receiving half as much money.
- The 14% deposit jump is textbook "savings repatriation": returnees bring home their stock of savings. This inflates measured inflows during a crisis even while the future remittance flow is being destroyed.

### Gaps
- Vande Bharat Mission total and Kerala-bound flight numbers were not fetched.
- The ₹5,000 one-time COVID relief to returnees, Dream Kerala, and Kerala's quarantine infrastructure: I found no sourced amounts, uptake or dates in this pass (the Azim Premji brief does not cover them).
- The PMC paper "Distress return migration amid COVID-19: Kerala's response" ([PMC9379595](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9379595/)) was blocked by a captcha. It likely has details on these schemes.

## 5. The remittance counter-cyclicality debate, and how Kerala's economy coped

### Takeaway
Measured inflows often hold up or even rise during crises, for three reasons: migrants repatriate their savings, the rupee depreciates, and returnees move money into NRE deposits. The COVID data (NRI deposits up 14% in FY21; over 70% of surveyed returnees said remittances rose) and the 2013 rupee slide are the clearest Kerala examples. The lagged effect is the real hit: a smaller emigrant stock, a slower remittance trend (after 2015), and an unabsorbed returnee pool.

### Cited Findings
- COVID: NRI deposits rose from ₹1.998 lakh cr to ₹2.274 lakh cr in FY21 as returnees deposited their savings. — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/economy-politics/story/nri-bank-deposits-to-kerala-rise-14-at-rs-227-lakh-crore-in-fy21-299939-2021-06-29); a Nov 2020 report likewise described Kerala bank deposits rising above ₹2 trillion despite COVID — [Onmanorama, 9 Nov 2020](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/business/2020/11/09/kerala-bank-coffers-swell-deposits-covid-19.html)
- Over 70% of returnees surveyed in 2020 said their monthly remittances increased after COVID. — [World Bank blog](https://blogs.worldbank.org/peoplemove/kerala-return-emigrant-survey-2021-insights-and-way-forward)
- 2013 rupee slide: NRE deposits rose as the rupee fell (headline: "Rupee slide effect: NRE deposits rise, remittances stay steady", Aug 2013). — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/finance/rupee-slide-effect-nre-deposits-rise-remittances-stay-steady-113080101447_1.html) (headline only). Also "NRI remittances surge to $7.18 bn in 2013" (Kerala) — [Business Standard, Mar 2014](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/economy-policy/nri-remittances-surge-to-7-18-bn-in-2013-114032800489_1.html) (headline only).
- Remittance growth slowed after 2015 partly because the rupee depreciated less. — [Rajan & Zachariah, IJLE 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41027-020-00280-z)

### Inferences
- For 2026: expect a possible short-term rise in measured inflows (savings brought home, a weak rupee, deposits parked in NRE accounts) that masks a later structural decline. Headline deposit or remittance data in the first 2–3 quarters will likely overstate resilience.
- A lesson that keeps not being learned: every episode (1990: <4,000 assisted; 2020: 14–15 lakh returnees, NDPREM ~1,200 beneficiaries/yr by 2023–24) shows reintegration schemes that are tiny relative to returnee volumes. Responses have been evacuation-led (Centre) and relief-led (state), not built around jobs or investment.

### Gaps
- I found no sourced Kerala-specific data on consumption, real estate or construction dips during any episode.
- No sourced Kerala Economic Review figures on sector impacts.
- The 2003 Iraq war and the 2017 Qatar blockade: I found nothing on their Kerala impact. Searches were not run for lack of budget, and the evidence I have suggests the effects were minor.
