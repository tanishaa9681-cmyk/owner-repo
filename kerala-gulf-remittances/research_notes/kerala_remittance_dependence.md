# Kerala's Economic Dependence on Gulf Remittances: Quantitative Profile (data through Sept 2026)

## 1. Kerala Migration Survey (KMS) 2023 and earlier rounds: emigrants, destinations, remittances, households, districts, communities

### Takeaway
KMS 2023 (IIMAD + GIFT, 20,000 households, the largest round so far) counts about 2.15 million emigrants. That is flat against 2018 and below the 2013 peak of 2.4 million. Total remittances, however, rose 2.5x to ₹2,16,893 crore, equal to 23.2% of NSDP. The Gulf still hosts 80.5% of emigrants, but its share is shrinking as student and Western migration grows. Emigration is concentrated in Malappuram and among Muslims.

### Cited Findings
**Emigrant stock by round (KMS):** 1998: 1.4 mn; 2003: 1.84 mn; 2008: 2.19 mn; 2013: 2.40 mn (peak); 2018: 2.12 mn (−11.6%); 2023: 2.15 mn (+1.5%). — [IIMAD KMS 2023 Report](https://iimad.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/KMS-2023-Report.pdf)
- Total Non-Resident Keralites (emigrants plus others counted as NRKs) were 4.0 million in 2023, up from 3.41 million in 2018 (KMS 2023). — [IIMAD KMS 2023](https://iimad.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/KMS-2023-Report.pdf)

**Destinations (2023):** GCC 80.5%, down from 89.2% in 2018, while non-GCC rose from 10.8% to 19.5%. By country: UAE 38.6%, Saudi Arabia 16.9%, Qatar 9.1%, Oman 6.4%, Kuwait 5.8%, Bahrain 3.7%. Outside the Gulf: UK 6.0%, Canada 2.5%, USA 2.2%, Australia 1.5%. — [IIMAD KMS 2023](https://iimad.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/KMS-2023-Report.pdf); [StudyIQ summary](https://www.studyiq.com/articles/kerala-migration-survey/)

**Remittances:**
- Total remittances were ₹2,16,893 crore in 2023, against ₹85,092 crore in 2018 (+154.9%). — [IIMAD KMS 2023](https://iimad.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/KMS-2023-Report.pdf)
- Household remittances were ₹37,058 crore in 2023, +20.6% from 2018. This is the portion received directly by households, as distinct from the total. — [IIMAD KMS 2023](https://iimad.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/KMS-2023-Report.pdf)
- Remittances per household were ₹2.24 lakh in 2023, against ₹96,185 in 2018. Per capita on the population base the figure is ₹61,118. — [StudyIQ summary of KMS 2023](https://www.studyiq.com/articles/kerala-migration-survey/)
- Remittances equalled 23.2% of NSDP in 2023, up from 13.5% in 2018. They were also 1.7x the state's revenue receipts. Kerala accounted for 21% of India's NRI deposits. — [IIMAD KMS 2023](https://iimad.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/KMS-2023-Report.pdf)
- Uses of remittances in 2023: house/shop renovation 15.8%, bank loan repayment 14.0%, education 10.0%. — [IIMAD KMS 2023](https://iimad.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/KMS-2023-Report.pdf)

**Household penetration:**
- 16.2% of households had at least one emigrant in 2023, down from 17.3% in 2018. — [IIMAD KMS 2023](https://iimad.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/KMS-2023-Report.pdf)
- "Nearly half" of surveyed households have experienced migration of some kind: current emigrants, returnees or internal migrants. — [IIMAD KMS 2023](https://iimad.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/KMS-2023-Report.pdf)

**Districts (emigrants, 2023):** Malappuram 3,77,647 (17.5%), Thrissur 2,33,177 (10.8%), Kannur 2,12,208 (9.9%), Kollam 1,95,815 (9.1%), Kozhikode 1,93,697 (9.0%). — [IIMAD KMS 2023](https://iimad.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/KMS-2023-Report.pdf)
- Remittance receipts are concentrated differently from emigrant counts: Kollam 17.8% (top recipient) and Malappuram 16.2%, attributed to KMS data. — [CPPR, 2026](https://www.cppr.in/archives/kerala-after-2026-gcc-crisis)

**Religion of emigrants (2023):** Muslim 41.9% (about 26% of the state's population), Hindu 35.2% (about 54% of population), Christian 22.3% (about 18% of population). — [IIMAD KMS 2023](https://iimad.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/KMS-2023-Report.pdf). Note: the [StudyIQ summary](https://www.studyiq.com/articles/kerala-migration-survey/) gives Hindus as "2%", which is clearly a typo. The 35.2% figure is from the primary report.

**Other characteristics:**
- Gender: 80.9% male, 19.1% female. — [IIMAD KMS 2023](https://iimad.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/KMS-2023-Report.pdf)
- Student emigrants rose from 1,29,763 (2018) to about 2.5 lakh (2023), 11.3% of emigrants. — [IIMAD KMS 2023](https://iimad.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/KMS-2023-Report.pdf)
- Return emigrants rose from 1.2 mn (2018) to 1.8 mn (2023). 36% of returnees came from the UAE, and the top reason for return was job loss (18.4%). — [IIMAD KMS 2023](https://iimad.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/KMS-2023-Report.pdf)
- About 1.4 million NRKs returned during COVID (2020–21). — [CPPR, 2026](https://www.cppr.in/archives/kerala-after-2026-gcc-crisis)
- Labour migrants make up 76.9% of emigrants. Occupations: sales 12.9%, transport 11.0%, construction 2.7%. — [CPPR, 2026](https://www.cppr.in/archives/kerala-after-2026-gcc-crisis)

### Inferences
- The number of emigrants has stagnated (2.15 mn in 2023 against 2.40 mn in 2013) while remittances have surged. The surge is therefore driven by higher earnings per migrant, rupee depreciation, better capture through formal channels and a changing skill mix, not by more migrants. Some of the 2018 to 2023 jump likely reflects methodology: 2023 was the first digital KMS with a larger sample.
- The GCC's 80.5% share of emigrants overstates the GCC share of remittance value, because the higher-earning emigrants to the UK, US and Canada are growing fastest. The 80% GCC-origin share of remittances cited by CPPR is probably an approximation that copies the emigrant share.

### Gaps
- District-wise remittance amounts in rupees were not retrieved. Only CPPR's percentages (Kollam, Malappuram) are available, and they were not verified against the primary tables.
- KMS remittance splits by GCC and non-GCC source country were not found.
- Remittances as % of NSDP for pre-2018 rounds were not retrieved. The widely cited historical range of about 20–35% of NSDP (roughly 30%+ in 2008–2013) is not sourced in these notes. See [Springer, Rajan & Zachariah 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41027-020-00280-z) for the 1972–2020 series; it was not fetched.

## 2. RBI remittance surveys: Kerala's share of India's inflows; GCC vs advanced economies

### Takeaway
In RBI surveys, Kerala's share of India's inward remittances fell from about 19% (2016-17) to 10.2% (2020-21), then rebounded to 19.7% in 2023-24, close to Maharashtra's 20.5%. At the national level the GCC's share of India's remittances has fallen from 46.7% to 37.9%, while advanced economies now supply more than half.

### Cited Findings
- **6th round (2023-24):** India's inward remittances were $118.7 bn. By state: Maharashtra 20.5%, Kerala 19.7%, Tamil Nadu 10.4%; the top three together took 50.6%. — [Onmanorama, May 2025, citing RBI Bulletin & SLBC](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/business/2025/05/29/nri-remittance-record-kerala-deposits.html); [FLAME/The Hindu, Apr 2026](https://www.flame.edu.in/in-the-media/kerala-likely-to-see-20-drop-in-remittances-as-west-asia-war-drags-on)
- **5th round (2020-21):** Kerala's share was 10.2%, a COVID-era low. — [Onmanorama 2025](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/business/2025/05/29/nri-remittance-record-kerala-deposits.html)
- Kerala's implied remittances were about $23.99 bn, or about ₹2 lakh crore a year. Remittances crossed ₹1 lakh crore/year around 2014 (about ₹8,333 crore a month) and now average about ₹16,665 crore a month. — [Onmanorama 2025](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/business/2025/05/29/nri-remittance-record-kerala-deposits.html)
- **Source countries (India, 2023-24):**
  - US 27.7%, UK 10.8%, Singapore 6.6%, Canada 3.8%, Australia 3.1%; advanced economies together exceed 50%.
  - GCC 37.9% (UAE 19.2%, Saudi Arabia 6.7%, Kuwait 3.9%).
  - The GCC share fell from 46.7% in 2016-17, and the UAE share from 26.9% in 2016-17. — [InsightsIAS summary of RBI Bulletin, Mar 2025](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2025/04/05/rbis-remittances-survey-2025/)
- CPPR (2026) describes the 10.2% to 19.7% move as Kerala's "share of India's GCC remittances". The RBI figures are shares of total inward remittances, so this appears to be a mislabel. — [CPPR](https://www.cppr.in/archives/kerala-after-2026-gcc-crisis)
- Kerala has about 3% of India's population but receives about 18–20% of its remittances. About 3–3.5 million of the estimated 18–19 million Indians in the GCC are Keralites. — [Dhanam Online, Mar 17 2026](https://english.dhanamonline.com/banking-and-finance/war-shrinks-gulf-remittances-to-india-kerala-feels-the-heat-9002932)

### Inferences
- Kerala's receipts are far more Gulf-weighted than India's: about 80% of its emigrants are in the GCC, against a 37.9% GCC share of national remittances. So a Gulf shock hits Kerala disproportionately even as India overall diversifies toward the US and UK.
- The 2020-21 drop to 10.2% partly reflects RBI's methodology of attributing remittances to the state where the receiving bank sits. Some of the rebound may be a shift in attribution and not only in flows.

### Gaps
- India's FY25 total (widely reported as about $129–135 bn, a record) was not verified from a primary source in this session.
- The 7th RBI survey round, if any was published in 2026, was not found.

## 3. Remittances as % of Kerala NSDP/GSDP; international comparison

### Takeaway
KMS 2023 puts remittances at 23.2% of NSDP (13.5% in 2018). Kerala's GSDP is budgeted at ₹14.27 lakh crore for 2025-26 and expected at ₹16.29 lakh crore for 2026-27. On those figures ₹2.17 lakh crore is about 13–15% of GSDP. That dependence is comparable to countries that rely heavily on remittances.

### Cited Findings
- Remittances were 23.2% of NSDP in 2023 and 13.5% in 2018. — [IIMAD KMS 2023](https://iimad.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/KMS-2023-Report.pdf)
- GSDP (budget estimate) 2025-26 is ₹14,27,145 crore. — [PRS Kerala Budget Analysis 2025-26](https://prsindia.org/budgets/states/kerala-budget-analysis-2025-26)
- Expected GSDP for 2026-27 is ₹16.29 lakh crore. — [FLAME/The Hindu, Apr 2026](https://www.flame.edu.in/in-the-media/kerala-likely-to-see-20-drop-in-remittances-as-west-asia-war-drags-on)
- Kerala's per capita GSDP in 2021-22 was ₹2,64,971, against ₹1,71,498 nationally (about 50% higher). — [NITI Aayog, Macro & Fiscal Landscape of Kerala, 2025](https://www.niti.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-03/Macro-and-Fiscal-Landscape-of-the-State-of-Kerala.pdf)
- A Substack commentary frames the 2026 Gulf war as threatening "23% of Kerala's income". It is a secondary source repeating the KMS figure. — [Bharatnama](https://bharatnama.substack.com/p/why-does-a-gulf-war-threaten-23-of)

### Inferences
- These are my calculations. ₹2,16,893 cr ÷ ₹14.27 lakh cr (2025-26 GSDP) ≈ 15.2%, and ÷ ₹16.29 lakh cr (2026-27) ≈ 13.3%. The ratio falls on GSDP, which is larger than NSDP, and as the economy grows.
- For comparison (not verified in this session; World Bank figures from memory): Nepal about 25–27% of GDP, the Philippines about 9%. That would place Kerala between the two. Verify before publishing.

### Gaps
- No primary World Bank figures were fetched for Nepal, the Philippines or Bangladesh.
- No historical series of remittances as % of NSDP (1998–2013) was fetched.

## 4. NRI deposits in Kerala banks (SLBC) and banking linkage

### Takeaway
NRI deposits in Kerala banks were ₹2,93,622 crore as of March 2025 and have since crossed ₹3 lakh crore. The state holds about 21% of India's NRI deposits. Nationally, a June 2026 RBI swap window drew record FCNR(B) inflows during the Gulf crisis.

### Cited Findings
- NRI deposits in Kerala banks were ₹2,93,622 crore as of March 2025 (SLBC) and have since crossed ₹3 lakh crore. — [Onmanorama, May 2025](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/business/2025/05/29/nri-remittance-record-kerala-deposits.html); [FLAME media: "heading to ₹3-trillion"](https://www.flame.edu.in/in-the-media/nri-deposits-in-kerala-banks-head-towards-rs-3-trillion-mark)
- NRI deposits were first reported above ₹1 lakh crore in 2015. — [Business Standard/IANS, Sept 2015](https://www.business-standard.com/article/news-ians/nri-deposits-in-kerala-banks-over-rs-1-lakh-crore-115092200680_1.html)
- Kerala's share of India's NRI deposits is 21%. — [IIMAD KMS 2023](https://iimad.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/KMS-2023-Report.pdf)
- **India NRI deposit stock:** $200.89 bn in July 2026 (FCNR(B) $68.28 bn, NRE $98.02 bn, NRO $34.59 bn), up from $167.86 bn in July 2025.
  - Apr–Jul FY27 inflows were $36.24 bn against $4.66 bn a year earlier. FCNR(B) alone brought $34.53 bn, driven by RBI's concessional swap facility introduced in June 2026, which mobilised $133 bn by Aug 31.
  - NRE inflows collapsed to $207 mn from $2.42 bn a year earlier. — [Business Standard, Sept 27 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/nri-deposit-flows-surge-over-6x-to-36-2-bn-in-apr-jul-fy27-rbi-data-126092700465_1.html)
- A separate source reports NRI deposit inflows down 23% in Q1 FY27. — [NRI Affairs](https://www.nriaffairs.com/nri-deposit-inflows-q1-fy27-rbi-data-2026/) (not fetched; it may conflict with, or be for a different window than, the Business Standard figures).

### Inferences
- The collapse of NRE inflows ($2.42 bn to $0.21 bn, Apr–Jul) is the flow most tied to ordinary Gulf worker savings. It is consistent with the 2026 Gulf shock squeezing worker remittances. FCNR(B) inflows came from arbitrage by wealthier NRIs and do not represent worker money.
- At about ₹3 lakh crore, NRI deposits are probably around a quarter to a third of total bank deposits in Kerala. Total deposits were not verified, so treat this as an estimate.

### Gaps
- The latest SLBC total deposits, NRI share of total and credit-deposit ratio (Kerala's C-D ratio has historically been about 60–65%, below the national average) were not retrieved.
- District-wise NRI deposits were not found.

## 5. Flow into state revenue and state finances (debt, fiscal stress)

### Takeaway
Remittance-led consumption supports Kerala's tax base. KMS says remittances are 1.7x the state's revenue receipts. Even so, Kerala's own tax revenue is only about 6.9% of GSDP, debt was about 38% of GSDP in 2022-23, and committed spending takes about 55% of expenditure. The state is exposed if Gulf money shrinks.

### Cited Findings
- Remittances equal 1.7x Kerala's revenue receipts (2023). — [IIMAD KMS 2023](https://iimad.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/KMS-2023-Report.pdf)
- Public debt was 38.2% of GSDP in 2022-23 (26.7% in 1990-91), and contingent liabilities 4.8% of GSDP in 2021-22. Own tax revenue was 6.9% of GSDP (2022-23). Committed expenditure was 8.9% of GSDP, or 54.9% of total expenditure. The debt ratio is unlikely to decline through 2026-27 in most scenarios. — [NITI Aayog, Mar 2025](https://www.niti.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-03/Macro-and-Fiscal-Landscape-of-the-State-of-Kerala.pdf)
- Kerala Budget 2025-26 (BE):
  - Receipts excluding borrowings ₹1,53,544 cr.
  - Fiscal deficit ₹45,039 cr (3.2% of GSDP); revenue deficit ₹27,125 cr (1.9%).
  - Gross borrowings ₹1,55,809 cr; debt repayment ₹1,14,961 cr. — [PRS 2025-26](https://prsindia.org/budgets/states/kerala-budget-analysis-2025-26)
- Aggregate debt is ₹5.07 lakh crore, and there is a projected revenue shortfall of ₹20,500 crore in 2026-27. — [CPPR, 2026](https://www.cppr.in/archives/kerala-after-2026-gcc-crisis)

### Inferences
- The link from remittances to revenue is indirect, because Kerala cannot tax remittances. The channels are:
  - SGST on consumption (a destination-based GST favours a consuming state like Kerala)
  - stamp duty and registration on land and housing
  - motor vehicle tax
  - sales tax on liquor and petrol
  - lottery receipts
- A 20% fall in remittances, about ₹43,000 cr, would hit these consumption-linked heads first.

### Gaps
- An itemised breakdown of own tax revenue (SGST, stamp duty, excise, MVT, KGST on liquor and fuel) and lottery revenue was not retrieved. Look in the PRS PDF, Kerala Budget in Brief or the CAG State Finances Audit Report 2023-24 ([CAG](https://cag.gov.in/webroot/uploads/download_audit_report/2025/Report-2023-24-Single-file-06902e4e81dd290.55444935.pdf)).
- No details were fetched on the borrowing-limit dispute with the Centre, including the Supreme Court case over the net borrowing ceiling and the counting of KIIFB/KSSPL off-budget borrowings.
- The latest outstanding liabilities and debt/GSDP for 2025-26 or 2026-27 (BE) were not verified. Commonly cited figures are about 34–37%.

## 6. Consumption economy indicators: consumption, housing, gold, migrant labour

### Takeaway
Kerala has the highest rural monthly per capita consumption expenditure (MPCE) among major states in HCES 2023-24 (₹6,611), and urban MPCE (₹7,783) is also well above the national average. About 11.9 lakh houses stood vacant in the 2011 Census, a symptom of remittance-built housing.

### Cited Findings
- HCES 2023-24, MPCE without imputation: Kerala rural ₹6,611 and urban ₹7,783, against all-India rural ₹4,122 and urban ₹6,996. Urban Kerala trails Telangana (₹8,978), Haryana (₹8,428) and Tamil Nadu (₹8,165). — [MoSPI HCES 2023-24 Press Note](https://www.mospi.gov.in/sites/default/files/press_release/HCES_Press_Note_2023-24_27122024_rev.pdf)
  - The extraction was garbled on rank. Kerala's rural ₹6,611 exceeds Punjab's (₹5,817), which makes Kerala the highest among major states. Sikkim is highest overall (rural ₹9,377, urban ₹13,927). — [Shankar IAS summary](https://www.shankariasparliament.com/current-affairs/gs-i/household-consumption-expenditure-survey-2023-24)
- Vacant houses in the 2011 Census numbered 11,89,144 (rural 5,85,998; urban 6,03,146), including 57,272 with 6+ rooms. The KSSP report of 2017 attributes part of this to owners living abroad and says the figure is now higher. — [The News Minute, 2017](https://www.thenewsminute.com/kerala/over-3000-people-are-homeless-kerala-while-almost-12-lakh-houses-lie-vacant-69932)
- The 2026/27 Census is making repeated visits before classifying houses as "vacant", which is relevant to future counts of locked houses. — [The Hindu via PressReader, Jun 19 2026](https://www.pressreader.com/india/the-hindu-kozhikode-9wwa/20260619/281621017041593) (headline only)
- The largest single use of remittances is house/shop renovation (15.8%). — [IIMAD KMS 2023](https://iimad.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/KMS-2023-Report.pdf)

### Inferences
- High consumption with modest local production is the "remittance economy" signature: consumption is financed by external income.

### Gaps
- The guest (interstate) worker count, commonly quoted as about 30–35 lakh (GIFT 2013 study projections), was not verified in this session. See [Wikipedia: Migrant labourers in Kerala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migrant_labourers_in_Kerala) and [Peoples Democracy 2025](https://peoplesdemocracy.in/2025/1005_pd/migrants-guests-kerala-experience) (not fetched).
- Kerala's gold consumption (often cited as about 20–25% of India's jewellery demand) was not sourced.

## 7. Listed companies and sectors with NRI/Gulf exposure

### Takeaway
Federal Bank's NRI deposits crossed ₹1 lakh crore in FY26. The other Kerala banks and gold lenders carry similar concentrations, but share-of-deposit figures were not verified this session.

### Cited Findings
- Federal Bank (Q1 FY27, Jun 2026): NRI deposits crossed ₹1 lakh crore in FY26, CASA crossed ₹1 lakh crore in FY26 and grew 18% YoY in Q1 FY27, and 85% of funding is "granular". Q1 FY27 net profit was ₹1,177 cr (+36.6%). — [InvestyWise, Sept 2026](https://www.investywise.com/federal-bank-investor-presentation-for-q1-fy27-results); [Multibagg](https://www.multibagg.ai/market-pulse/articles/federal-q1fy27-1059940613)
- Sectors exposed to a GCC shock:
  - Kerala exposure: sales, transport and construction occupations among migrants.
  - Gulf-based Kerala businesses: retail (Lulu), gold (Malabar, Joyalukkas), healthcare (Aster). — [CPPR, 2026](https://www.cppr.in/archives/kerala-after-2026-gcc-crisis) (qualitative)

### Inferences
- Federal Bank's total deposits are about ₹2.9–3 lakh crore (not verified), so ₹1 lakh crore+ of NRI deposits implies roughly 30–35% of deposits. That would make it the listed bank most exposed to Kerala-Gulf flows. Verify from the investor presentation.

### Gaps
- No verified NRI share of deposits for South Indian Bank, CSB Bank or Dhanlaxmi, and no inward remittance market share for Federal Bank (historically claimed at about 20% of India's inward personal remittances).
- The Gulf and NRI business share of Muthoot Finance and Manappuram was not retrieved.
- Gulf revenue shares of listed Gulf-linked companies (e.g., Aster DM, Kalyan Jewellers Middle East) were not fetched.

- NDPREM, the returnee reintegration scheme, had 1,200 beneficiaries and ₹18 cr of spending in FY24, which is tiny against the returnee stock of 1.8 mn. — [CPPR](https://www.cppr.in/archives/kerala-after-2026-gcc-crisis)
